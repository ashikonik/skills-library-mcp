# Structural & Stylistic AI Patterns (Detailed Reference)

Word choice is only half the problem. These are the structural, grammatical, and tonal habits that give AI writing away even when the vocabulary looks fine. Severity 5 = fix immediately, no exceptions. Severity 2-3 = watch for it, use judgment.

---

## Punctuation & Grammar

**Em dashes (severity 5).** AI drops them into sentences constantly—like this—for pauses, emphasis, or drama. Real writers use them sparingly. Cap it at one per 500 words, max. Default to a comma, a period, or restructuring the sentence instead.

**Dramatic ellipses (severity 3).** "And then... silence..." — AI uses these for false suspense. Use a period, or cut the sentence entirely, unless you're genuinely showing trailed-off speech.

**Curly/smart quotes (severity 2).** Usually a formatting artifact, not a writing issue, but it's a known tell in copy-pasted AI text. Not something to obsess over in a rewrite, but worth knowing.

**Suspiciously perfect grammar (severity 3).** No fragments, no comma splices, no sentences starting with "And" or "But," Oxford comma every time, zero typos, rare contractions. Real human writing breaks its own rules sometimes. Let a sentence fragment stand if it hits harder. Use contractions ("it's," "don't," "we've") — AI underuses them.

## Overused Constructions

**"Not X, but Y" / negative parallelism (severity 4).**
- "It's not just a storm, but a tempest."
- "This isn't about efficiency, it's about survival."
- "Not merely tired, but exhausted."
Pick one description. Say it once, directly.

**Rhetorical question → immediate answer (severity 4).**
- "What did she feel? Everything and nothing."
- "The result? Chaos."
- "Why? Because it mattered."
This is a crutch for fake profundity. Either ask a real question and let it sit, or just make the statement.

**Rule of three (severity 5).** AI defaults to triplets even when a triplet isn't earned.
- "She was smart, capable, and determined."
- "Innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking."
- "He stopped, he stared, he listened."
Vary it. Use one item, two, or four. Three should only show up when it's genuinely the right number, not a reflex.

**Forced sass / hot-take voice (severity 4).** Common on LinkedIn-style AI writing:
- "But here's the thing:"
- "Then I realized:"
- "The result?"
- "Hot take:"
- "And honestly?"
- "Here's why that matters."
- "Here's the deal."
- "That's the real unlock."
These manufacture edge and confidence that isn't earned by the actual content. Cut them and just say the thing.

**"No X. No Y. Just Z." (severity 4).** A minimalist-sounding AI cadence. "No fluff. No filler. Just results." Say what you mean in one sentence instead.

**False range ("from X to Y") (severity 3).**
- "from strategic planning to day-to-day execution"
- "from small startups to Fortune 500 companies"
Sounds comprehensive, says nothing specific. Either name the actual range concretely or drop it.

## Structural Tells

**Bullet-point addiction (severity 4).** AI breaks narrative or conversational text into bullets even when a sentence would do. Bullets belong in genuine lists (steps, comparisons, options) — not as a substitute for a well-written paragraph.

**Section headers in short-form writing (severity 3).** "### Understanding the Problem," "### The Solution," "### Moving Forward" — rigid, textbook-like scaffolding. Fine for a long report; a giveaway in an email, post, or essay.

**Preamble syndrome (severity 3).** "Let's break it down." "Let's dive into this." "Let's dissect this topic." Just start with the actual content.

**Uniform paragraph length (severity 3).** AI writes paragraphs that are all roughly the same length, like it's filling a template. Human writing varies — some paragraphs are one line, some run long.

**The bloated, repetitive conclusion (severity 4).** AI conclusions are often the longest part of the piece and mostly just restate what was already said, usually starting with "Overall," "In conclusion," or "In summary." Human conclusions are short, or nonexistent. If a piece doesn't need a wrap-up sentence, don't add one.

## Tone & Voice Issues

**Excessive positivity / conflict avoidance (severity 3).** AI defaults to diplomatic, balanced, non-committal takes — "there are pros and cons to consider" — even when a real person would just pick a side. If the user has a clear opinion in their original text, keep the edge. Don't sand it down into balance for balance's sake.

**Earnest self-congratulation about being helpful (severity 3).** Phrasing that draws attention to its own helpfulness or thoroughness. Cut it — helpfulness should show up in the content, not be announced.

**Vagueness that could apply to any topic (severity 4).** AI writes to avoid being wrong or off-topic, which makes it generic. If a sentence could be pasted into a different piece on a totally different subject and still make sense, it's too vague — make it specific to what's actually being discussed.

---

## Specificity Problems

**Avoiding real proper nouns (severity 4).** AI defaults to generic placeholders instead of specific, concrete names, places, or products. When a real name, number, or detail is available, use it — don't default to vague description.

**"AI names" (severity 3).** When AI invents a first name for an example, it defaults overwhelmingly to "Emily" or "Sarah." If you're generating an example name, don't reach for those — use something else.

**Generic metaphors and comparisons (severity 4).**
- "a symphony of colors"
- "a tapestry of emotions"
- "a delicate balance"
- "nestled in"
These show up regardless of context because they're pattern-matched, not chosen for the specific thing being described. Use a concrete, specific description instead, or cut the metaphor entirely.

**Unmotivated/nonsensical metaphors (severity 5).** Occasionally AI reaches for "creative" comparisons that don't actually track: "her voice was like sandpaper on silk." If a metaphor doesn't clarify meaning, don't use it.

**Telling instead of showing (severity 4).** Explaining a reaction right after describing it: "She was angry, which showed in her expression." "...which was surprising because he hadn't expected it." Cut the explanatory tail — let the action or the fact stand on its own.

**Redundant adjective pairs (severity 3).** "dark and brooding," "loud and brash," "quick and agile." Pick the one word that's actually doing the work.

---

## Repetition & Consistency

**Self-repetition (severity 3).** AI tends to restate the same point in slightly different words within the same piece, especially in conclusions. Say it once.

**Abrupt tonal shifts (severity 3).** When AI text gets mixed with human edits, the seams show — a casual paragraph followed by a suddenly formal one, or vice versa. Keep one consistent voice throughout a rewrite.

**Excessive emphasis (severity 3).** Bold and italics used to manufacture importance rather than because the sentence structure calls for it. "This isn't just *important* — it's **revolutionary**." Rely on word choice and sentence structure for emphasis, not formatting.

---

## Quick-Reference Severity 5 List (fix these first, always)

1. Em dash overuse
2. "Delve" and academic-register filler words generally
3. "Not X, but Y" constructions
4. Rule of three used reflexively
5. Unmotivated/nonsensical metaphors
